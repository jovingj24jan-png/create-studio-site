Overview

Blackwell sold cars the way most makers do: a brochure site, a lead form, and a showroom that had to repeat everything from scratch.

We rebuilt the front end of the business as one system. A configurator that renders in the browser, a spec language shared by the website and the showroom screens, and a handover that carries a customer's saved build into the conversation they have in person.

The work covered the brand system, the product design and the build. It shipped in fourteen weeks across two markets.

Configurator and the shared spec system

The brief

Make choosing a car feel like the car.

Blackwell's cars are quiet, precise and slightly severe. Their digital presence was none of those things. The brief was less about screens than about temperament — the site had to carry the same restraint as the product.

Challenge

Three systems that disagreed with each other.

Pricing lived in one place, availability in another, and the showroom ran a third. A configuration a customer built at home could not survive the trip to a dealer, so every conversation restarted.

The rendering was the visible half of the problem. The harder half was agreeing on a single description of a car that all three systems would accept.

Solution

One spec language, rendered everywhere.

We defined the car as a single structured spec and made every surface a renderer of it. The browser configurator draws from it in WebGL, the showroom display reads the same payload, and a saved build resolves to a short code a salesperson can type.

Search runs on embeddings, so a customer asking for "something quiet for long motorway runs" lands on a real trim rather than an empty result.
