Overview

Lindholm had built a bike that goes 877 kilometres on a charge. What they did not have was a reason for anyone to believe it.

We named the bike after the number and then built the entire launch around proving it. The site opens on the range figure and spends the rest of its length earning it — route data, charge cycles, and the testing conditions written out in full.

Identity, launch site and the paid social cut all shipped together in nine weeks.

Identity and the range evidence

The brief

Make the number impossible to dismiss.

Every e-bike brand claims range, and every buyer has learned to halve whatever they read. The brief was not to shout 877 louder. It was to show the working.

Challenge

A category where nobody trusts the spec sheet.

Range claims in e-mobility are quoted under conditions no rider will ever meet. Lindholm's number was real and measured, which made it worse — it looked like everyone else's marketing.

The launch also had to run in paid social, where the whole argument has to survive being cut to six seconds.

Solution

Put the test conditions next to the claim.

The range figure never appears without its conditions attached — rider weight, terrain, assist level, temperature. A scroll-linked route map draws the actual test ride as you read, so the number resolves into a journey rather than a boast.

The same asset set was cut down for paid social, where the first frame is the route and the claim lands second.
